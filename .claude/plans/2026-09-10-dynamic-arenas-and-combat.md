# Plan: Dynamic arenas, abilities, and tactical personalities

**Goal:** Add continuously roaming hills, shared cliff/road terrain, charge and timing-sensitive shield/stun, room movement controls, and configurable tactical opponents through shared authoritative mechanics.
**Out of scope:** Deployment/push, finishing prior human/live race playtests, 3D/jumping, held-shield stamina, new account/host roles, LLM/RL bots, arbitrary terrain editors, rollback networking, and camera redesign.
**Status:** Steps 1 and 2 complete on 2026-09-10. ADR 0008 is accepted; the unused swept-geometry foundation is verified on both advisory Mac/Docker lanes with the accepted fixtures unchanged. Step 3 is next. The physics prototype gate (Step 5) must pass before Phase C. Renumbered by the 2026-09-10 design review into three phases; Phase C is provisional until Step 5 passes.

### Facts verified in the tree by the 2026-09-10 design review

- **A contact response cannot see gameplay state.** `ContactRule::Response` receives two `Subject`s, the contact, and a `TickContext` that exposes only the tick, the delta, the simulation config, the map, and the spatial index (`src/simulation/contact_rule.hpp`, `src/simulation/tick_context.hpp:79-92`). Predicates read the committed world; responses do not. The only way gameplay state reaches a response today is which row's predicates matched, which is the flag-combination table ADR 0008 rejects. `ContactResponse` can replace two bodies and emit events, nothing else; it has no motion termination.
- **A mode has seven declarations, read once at construction** (`src/simulation/game_mode.hpp`, `src/simulation/game_simulation_setup.hpp:30-39`). ADR 0004 § "The tick" and ADR 0003 § "Canonical tick" both state the kernel has exactly two policy sockets and no third. A motion-trigger socket is therefore a versioned amendment to both, plus an eighth declaration.
- **Thrust maximum is authored once per mode section**, `[royale]`, `[king_of_the_hill]`, and `[race]` (`src/application/application_config_loader.cpp:118,124,135`), validated by three configurations, and passed to three `ThrustSteeringSystem::create(...)` calls. The key also appears in `config/blob-royale.cfg`, `deploy/ubuntu-pc/blob-royale.cfg`, every `frontend-react/e2e/fixtures/*.cfg`, every `tests/fixtures/replays/*/match.ini`, the fuzz corpus under `tests/fuzz/corpus/application/`, `tests/integration/server_process_fixture.cpp`, and `tests/unit/application/application_input_test_fixture.hpp`. `[simulation] drag_per_second` is the precedent for a physical parameter authored once below every mode.
- **One random generator.** `GameWorld::random()` is a single SplitMix64 seeded from the match seed; hazards draw from it (`src/gameplay/shared/hazard_spawn_system.cpp:60`) and the snapshot publishes one `random_draw_count` (`src/simulation/world_snapshot.hpp:76`). Every bot receives the same match seed (`src/application/bot_reconciliation.cpp:110`).
- **The race road is copied every tick.** `CoursePublisherSystem` stamps `track` and `track_half_width` into `RaceModeState` at `kLifecycle`; the client draws from that copy (`frontend-react/src/features/simulation/rendering/raceCourseRenderer.ts`), and the racer bot re-implements the nearest-segment projection (`src/controllers/racer_controller.cpp:24`) because nothing below gameplay owns it (`src/gameplay/race/race_course.cpp:79`).
- **Body-bound cleanup is per owner.** Respawn erases only the `PhysicsBody` and knows no other kind; each owner clears its own counter (`src/gameplay/shared/respawn_system.hpp`). `c84e2fb` fixed the hill's missed clear on the knockout tick (`src/gameplay/king_of_the_hill/hill_scoring_system.cpp`, pinned by `tests/unit/gameplay/king_of_the_hill/hill_return_timing_tests.cpp`). Royale needs no clear because `placement_recorder` destroys the entity. `ComponentPublication<T>` (`src/simulation/component_publication.hpp`) is the model for a per-kind trait the registry consumes generically.
- **Timers count down.** `RespawnTimer` and `Lifetime` decrement with a write per tick; nothing yet expresses an absolute-tick half-open window.
- **Hill motion holds no committed state.** `hill_center` is a pure function of the markers and the elapsed running ticks (`src/gameplay/king_of_the_hill/hill_geometry.hpp`); random roam needs committed velocity, retarget tick, and stream state.
- **Seating occupancy uses one common radius and `std::hypot`** (`src/simulation/spawn_seating.cpp:15`); every other geometry in the tree writes out `sqrt(dx*dx + dy*dy)` for cross-toolchain agreement.
- **Controllers hold exactly two capabilities** and see only what the snapshot carries (`src/controllers/README.md`); terrain reaches a bot only through the published snapshot value.
- **Welcome carries static session facts** (`lobby_id`, `seat_count_maximum`, `npc_controller_kinds`; `src/protocol/session_welcome.hpp:54-63`), which is the precedent for room data that never changes during a session.
- **Test names** are `unit.<domain>.<test case>`, `fixtures.<test case>`, and `integration.<name>`; `ctest -R` matches test-case text, so every filter below uses a domain prefix rather than a word from a test name.
- **Review artifacts** live at `docs/reviews/<date>-<slug>-review.md`.

## Execution constraints

- **The accepted baseline does not move before Step 16.** Every wall, pair, replay, and deployment fixture and the accepted-baseline oracle pass untouched after every step of Phases A and B. A step in those phases that changes one committed value stops and surfaces. Fixture configurations author an explicit normal-speed ceiling no fixture reaches, so Step 10 changes no committed value either.
- **Both lanes, every C++ step.** Every `verify-focused` line is run on `linux-gcc-debug` and on `linux-clang-asan-ubsan`, one build at a time, and both are written out below. The client runs through `./scripts/verify-web` and the browser gate through `./scripts/verify-browser-e2e`, each inside `./scripts/run-linux-toolchain`.
- **One step per commit**, with the step name as the subject and an execution note in this file for every departure. Stage explicit paths.
- **Promotions are pure moves.** Arithmetic that moves to a canonical module keeps its written operation order byte for byte, and a test proves the old and new answers identical before any reader switches. A behavior change is a separate commit with the test that fails without it.
- **Gate rule.** Step 5 blocks Phase C only. Phase B does not depend on the solver and may proceed while Step 5 awaits the owner. Phase C is provisional: if the gate changes the motion model, amend Steps 16 through 24 and ADR 0008 before executing them; do not guess through them.
- **Wire rule.** Session v3 is one major. The version const moves once, in Step 7, and every later kind, block, member, and command lands under 3.0 in the step whose authoritative behavior it carries, with its schema, generated client types, client validation, examples, and the row in `docs/protocol/v3.md` in that same commit. No vocabulary is reserved ahead of behavior, and no command is advertised in `welcome` before its server handler exists. Development commits are not deployable releases of v3; the release ships server and assets together after Step 24.
- **Kernel seams change only in the steps that name them**: Step 2 (root arithmetic and event order), Step 9 (random streams), Step 13 (component lifetime trait), and Step 16 (solver, response signature, motion disposition, trigger declaration). A feature step that needs a kernel change it does not find stops and amends this file rather than adding it inline.
- **ADR lockstep.** A step that changes a stated contract amends the named ADR section in the same commit with a dated entry. ADR 0008 records the Step 1 decisions before any dependent step runs.
- **Body-bound rule.** A component that exists only while a body exists declares the Step 13 trait and nothing else; no system writes its own clear after Step 13.
- **Timer rule.** Every ability window is the Step 14 tick-window value stored as absolute ticks; no new countdown field.
- **Root rule.** Every event time, contact or trigger, comes from the Step 2 module under the one event-order policy; no second root solver or comparator anywhere.
